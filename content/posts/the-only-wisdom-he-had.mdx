I typed a question into the AI. Got the answer in two seconds. Correct, complete, done. I felt nothing.

---

Socrates never claimed to know anything. He said the only wisdom he had over other people was knowing he lacked it. His whole method was asking questions until the person across from him realized their confidence was empty.

He never wrote a word down. The insight was never the record. It was the asking.

---

For most of history, knowing things was the scarce skill. Memorize the answer, hold the answer, you were valuable. That scarcity is gone. A model holds more facts than any person who ever lived.

*What's scarce now is the question that makes the facts matter.*

---

I used to think intelligence looked like having answers ready. Now I think it looks like Socrates in the agora, annoying everyone, refusing to let a good-enough answer pass as a true one.

---

Curiosity isn't a soft skill anymore. It's the whole skill. The one thing a fast, confident answer can't do for you is ask the next question.
